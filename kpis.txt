OVERVIEW PAGE — COMPLETE EXPLANATION
=====================================
Everything shown on the Overview tab, what it means, and why it matters.
No technical jargon. Written so you can explain it to anyone tomorrow.


════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PART 1 — THE ANNUAL BUDGET PANEL  (collapsible block at the top)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This block gives a 30-second health check for the whole firm for the current year.


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Active Clients
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What it shows:
  The total number of clients who have a signed budget for this year.

Why it matters:
  It tells you how many relationships you are actively managing.
  If this number changes unexpectedly, someone added or removed a contract.


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. YTD Consumed  (Year To Date Consumed)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What it shows:
  The total number of hours your team has actually worked and logged
  across ALL clients, from January 1st up to today.

Plain example:
  If it is May and your team has worked 1 200 hours so far this year,
  this number says 1 200h.

Why it matters:
  It is your real workload meter. Compare it to the total budget to know
  if you are ahead, behind, or on pace.


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. YTD Client Gain  (Year To Date Client Gain)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What it shows:
  The difference between what all clients were billed for so far this year
  and what your team actually consumed.

  Positive (green)  = you worked fewer hours than you billed → profit margin
  Negative (red)    = you worked more hours than you billed  → you are losing

How it is calculated:
  For each client:
    (Hours per month on contract × number of months elapsed) − hours consumed so far

  Then add all clients together.

Plain example:
  A client pays for 10h per month. It is May (4 months elapsed = January to April).
  Your team worked 35h for them.
  Their contribution: (10 × 4) − 35 = 40 − 35 = +5h gain.
  Repeat for every client. The sum is the total YTD gain.

Why it matters:
  This is the single most important profitability number for the year so far.
  If it is deeply negative, you are burning more capacity than you are billing.


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. This Month's Hours
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What it shows:
  The total hours logged by the whole team in the current calendar month.

Why it matters:
  A quick pulse check. If the number is unusually low mid-month,
  timesheets may not have been submitted yet.


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. Pace Health — On Track / At Risk / Over Budget  (the three coloured counters)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What it shows:
  Every client gets a traffic-light colour based on how fast the team is
  consuming their internal hour budget compared to plan.

How the colour is decided (the "pace" of consumption):
  Green  — the team is consuming at or below 85% of the planned internal pace  → all good
  Yellow — the team is consuming between 85% and 100% of the planned pace     → watch closely
  Red    — the team is consuming faster than 100% of the planned pace         → already over

Plain example:
  You planned 10h/month for a client internally.
  Your team actually worked an average of 11h/month.
  That is 110% → Red.

The counters simply tell you how many clients fall in each colour category.

Why it matters:
  Red count is the most urgent number. Every red client is a project where
  the team is burning capacity faster than the contract allows.


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. Timesheet Submission Progress  (the progress bar)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What it shows:
  How many collaborators have already submitted their timesheet for this month
  vs how many need to.

  Example: "3/7" means 3 have submitted, 4 have not.

Why it matters:
  Until a timesheet is submitted, the consumed hours for that person are not
  counted in any of the calculations above. If many people are pending,
  all the YTD numbers are understated.

If there are pending people, their names are shown so you can follow up.


════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PART 2 — THE THREE KPI CARDS  (the row of summary boxes below the panel)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
KPI 1 — Clients in Budget Overrun  (red card)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What it shows:
  The number of clients who are currently in the red zone:
  either their burn rate exceeds 100% OR they have already consumed
  more hours than they have been billed for year to date.

Why it matters:
  This is your immediate alert count. Zero is the goal.
  Each client counted here needs a conversation or a corrective action.


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
KPI 2 — Active Collaborators  (blue card)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What it shows:
  The total number of staff members tracked in the system
  (collaborators and workers, not admins).

  If a sub-line appears saying "X at burnout risk", that means
  X people have been working at an unusually high pace recently.

Why it matters:
  Keeps the headcount visible. The burnout warning is an early signal
  that workload needs to be redistributed.


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
KPI 3 — Total YTD Gain  (green card)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What it shows:
  The combined YTD gain across the top 10 most profitable clients specifically
  (not all clients — just the profitable ones from the top 10 rentable table).

  Positive = those clients generated saved capacity so far this year.

Why it matters:
  It gives you a quick sense of how much "buffer" your best-performing
  clients are providing.


════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PART 3 — TOP 10 MOST PROFITABLE CLIENTS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This table answers: "Which clients are we making the most margin on?"

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HOW THE RANKING IS DECIDED
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Clients are ranked by their "Gain YTD" from highest to lowest.
The client at #1 has the biggest positive gap between what they were billed
for and what was actually worked — meaning the most hours "saved" on their contract.

If two clients had the same gain, the one that appears first in the database
would be ranked higher (in practice this is rare).


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COLUMN BY COLUMN
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Rank (#1, #2 …)
  Position in the ranking. #1 is the most profitable client this year so far.
  The number is displayed in gold.

Client
  The client's name as registered in the annual budget.

Superviseur (Supervisor)
  The main person responsible for this client relationship internally.

Client H/an  (Client Hours per Year)
  How many hours the client is paying for per year, as written in the contract.

  How it is calculated:
    Monthly contracted hours × 12

  Plain example:
    A client paying for 40h/month → Client H/an = 480h.

  Why it matters:
    This is the ceiling. Your team must not exceed this number by the end of the year,
    or you are working for free.

Consommé YTD  (Consumed Year To Date)
  The actual hours your team has worked for this client from January up to now.

  Plain example:
    It is May. Your team has logged 11h for this client across the year.
    Consommé YTD = 11h.

Gain YTD  (Gain Year To Date)  ← THE RANKING COLUMN
  The profit margin in hours so far this year.

  How it is calculated:
    (Monthly contract hours × number of months elapsed) − hours consumed so far

  Plain example:
    Contract: 40h/month.  4 months elapsed.  11h consumed.
    Gain YTD = (40 × 4) − 11 = 160 − 11 = +149h

  Green number = you are ahead (profitable).
  Red number   = you are behind (losing).

  Why it matters:
    This is the core profitability signal per client.
    A large positive gain means the team is very efficient on this contract.

Rythme moyen  (Average Pace)
  How fast the team is consuming the INTERNAL hour budget on average,
  expressed as a percentage.

  How it is calculated:
    Each month: hours worked that month ÷ internal hours planned for that month
    Then average across all elapsed months.

  Colour meaning:
    Green  (below 85%)   → well under control
    Yellow (85% to 100%) → approaching the limit, watch it
    Red    (above 100%)  → already over the internal budget

  Important distinction:
    The "Client H/an" column is what the CLIENT pays for.
    The internal hours used in "Rythme moyen" is B2A's OWN internal estimate of how
    many hours it should realistically take. It may be lower than the client contract,
    which is intentional — it creates a buffer for profit.

  Plain example:
    Internal plan: 6h/month.  January: 0,6h worked.  Pace = 0,6/6 = 10%.
    February: 0h.  March: 0h.  April: 10,4h.  Pace = 10,4/6 = 173%.
    Average pace over 4 months = (10% + 0% + 0% + 173%) / 4 ≈ 46%.
    Displayed as: 46% (green — well under overall).

PART 4 — TOP 10 BUDGET OVERRUN CLIENTS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This table answers: "Which clients are bleeding the most hours?"

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO APPEARS IN THIS TABLE
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A client appears here if they meet at least ONE of these conditions:
  - Their average pace is RED (consuming faster than 100% of internal plan)
  - Their Gain YTD is negative (they have already consumed more than billed)

This means a client can appear here even before a real financial loss,
as a warning that the trajectory is bad.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HOW THE RANKING IS DECIDED
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Ranked by Gain YTD from LOWEST to highest (most negative first).
The client at #1 is the one with the worst overrun — the biggest gap between
what was billed and what was consumed, in the wrong direction.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COLUMN BY COLUMN
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Rank (#1, #2 …)
  Position in the overrun ranking. #1 is the worst situation.
  The number is displayed in red.

Client
  The client name.

Superviseur (Supervisor)
  The person responsible for this client.

Client H/mois  (Client Hours per Month)
  How many hours the client contracts for PER MONTH (not per year).
  This is the raw monthly figure from the contract.

  Note: unlike the profitable table which shows the annual total,
  the overrun table uses the monthly figure to make the overrun
  more visually obvious at a glance.

Consommé YTD  (Consumed Year To Date)
  Same as in the profitable table — actual hours worked since January.

Dépassement  (Overrun)
  The same "Gain YTD" number but shown here because it is negative.
  It tells you by how many hours you are already over what was billed.

  Plain example:
    Client H/mois = 8h.  5 months elapsed.  52h consumed.
    Billed so far = 8 × 5 = 40h.
    Dépassement = 40 − 52 = −12h.
    Meaning: the team has already worked 12 hours more than the client paid for.

  The number is always shown in red here.

Rythme moyen  (Average Pace)
  Same calculation as in the profitable table.
  Here the colour is always red or amber because these clients
  are already in a problematic range.


════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PART 5 — PROFITABILITY BY MANAGER  (the cards at the bottom)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This section answers: "Which supervisor is managing the most profitable portfolio?"

Each card represents one supervisor and summarises ALL the clients they are
responsible for combined.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT EACH CARD SHOWS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Supervisor name
  The person responsible for those clients.

YTD Gain Total  (the big number in the centre)
  The sum of YTD gains across all of that supervisor's clients.
  Green = their portfolio is profitable overall.
  Red   = their portfolio is consuming more than billed overall.

Average Pace bar
  The average pace across all their clients combined, with the same traffic-light
  colour logic (green/yellow/red). The bar fills from left to right —
  a fully filled red bar means the portfolio is at or over 100% pace.

Clients
  How many clients that supervisor is responsible for.

Clients in Overrun  (e.g. "2/5")
  Out of their total clients, how many are currently in the red or
  have a negative gain. If zero, all their clients are healthy.

Consommé YTD
  Total hours worked across all their clients year to date.

Overrun Rate  (Taux de Dépassement)
  The percentage of their clients that are in overrun.

  How it is calculated:
    (Number of clients in overrun ÷ total clients) × 100

  Plain example:
    Supervisor has 5 clients. 2 are in overrun. Overrun rate = 40%.

  Why it matters:
    A manager with a high overrun rate needs to look at how they are
    allocating the team's time across their portfolio.


════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
QUICK REFERENCE — WHAT EACH COLOUR MEANS EVERYWHERE
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Green  → Good. Below 85% of internal pace. Comfortable margin.
Yellow → Watch out. Between 85% and 100% of internal pace. No room for error.
Red    → Problem. Above 100% of internal pace. The team is over-consuming.

For gain numbers:
  + (positive, green) → profitable, worked fewer hours than billed
  − (negative, red)   → losing, worked more hours than billed


════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
THE KEY CONCEPT IN ONE PARAGRAPH
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Every client has two hour budgets:
  1. What the CLIENT pays for (the contract) — this is the revenue ceiling.
  2. What B2A internally plans to spend (always lower) — this is the cost target.

"Gain YTD" measures how far you are from the revenue ceiling.
"Rythme moyen / Average Pace" measures how far you are from the cost target.

A client can be green on pace (team working efficiently) and still have a
small gain if the contract hours are generous. A client can also be red on
pace but still profitable if the contract had a lot of buffer hours.

The ideal client: high gain YTD (lots of hours billed but not consumed)
AND green pace (team is working well within the internal budget).
